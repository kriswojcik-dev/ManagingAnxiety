Myths and Realities about Anxiety

Misunderstandings about anxiety can keep people from getting the help they need. Here are eight of the most common myths, with facts that reveal the truth.

Common Myths and Facts

  • Myth You can faint or lose control during a panic attack. Fact Panic raises blood pressure slightly; fainting is unlikely.
  • Myth People with anxiety should avoid stressful situations. Fact Avoidance reinforces anxiety. Facing fears helps reduce it.
  • Myth Anxiety is only a problem for adults. Fact About 9.4% of U.S. children also live with diagnosed anxiety.
  • Myth Anxiety and depression are unrelated. Fact Many people experience both, and anxiety is linked to other conditions too.
  • Myth Medication is the only treatment. Fact Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) is as effective—or more effective—for many people.
  • Myth Anxiety medications are addictive. Fact SSRIs and SNRIs are not addictive. Only some medications, like benzodiazepines, can cause dependence.
  • Myth A healthy lifestyle will cure anxiety. Fact Exercise, diet, and sleep help, but they don’t cure anxiety disorders without treatment.
  • Myth Constant reassurance from loved ones is the best support. Fact Too much reassurance can reinforce fears. Encouragement to face anxiety is more helpful.
